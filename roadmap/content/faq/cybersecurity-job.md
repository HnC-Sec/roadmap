Most people start in a general IT role, like help desk or a system administrator job, and move into cybersecurity from there. Very few people land directly in a cybersecurity job with no IT experience first.

Once you have some IT experience, common entry points into cybersecurity include incident response (investigating and containing attacks after they happen), threat hunting (proactively looking for attackers already inside a network), and security analyst roles in a Security Operations Center (SOC). These jobs pay well and are usually offered by private companies, either as a direct employee or as a contractor.

> [!TIP]
> Working for a company directly is usually an easier way to start than contracting. Contract work comes with a lot of extra considerations (finding your own clients, handling your own taxes and benefits, inconsistent income) that are much easier to deal with once you already have experience.

Wherever you're aiming, the [roadmap]({{< ref "/roadmap" >}}) on this site lays out the skills you'll need to build along the way.
